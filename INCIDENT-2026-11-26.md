# Incident report: 26 Nov 2026, 02:11 — out of memory, the desktop fell over

Timeline, cause, damage and what came back. Written right after the
recovery, from the journals on this machine.

Written by: vibe (2026-11-26)

## The basics

| | |
| --- | --- |
| When | Thu 26 Nov 2026, 02:11:04–02:11:16 GMT |
| What | The kernel OOM killer killed processes; Hyprland then crashed and restarted |
| Impact | Every agent window and the Grok Bot app died; the compositor came back in fallback ("safe mode"); all seats went `away` |
| Loss | None to the deck: notes queue on disk. Grok was killed mid-session and was told to check its work tree |
| Back online | 02:13–02:16 (all non-spent seats), chats reopened ~02:13, self-test `all passed` |

## Timeline

- **02:11:04** — kitty faults in a page during an allocation and invokes the
  OOM killer. At that moment: ~7.1 GiB of the 7.5 GiB RAM is anonymous or
  unevictable, page cache is at 159 672 pages, and **there is no swap at
  all** (`Free swap = 0kB, Total swap = 0kB`).
- **02:11:04–02:11:11** — a cascade of OOM kills: three `python3` helpers
  (deck hooks/status lines inside agent terminals) and, notably,
  **grok-bot** (pid 1750, the Grok Bot app).
- **02:11:05–02:11:11** — Hyprland's clients die out from under it;
  `DRM_IOCTL_ASAHI_VM_BIND failed` / "BO creation failed" (the Asahi GPU
  driver cannot get memory — this iMac shares RAM with the GPU);
  kitty core-dumps in `glfwSwapBuffers`.
- **02:11:11** — **Hyprland crashes** (SIGABRT), leaving its crash report
  at `~/.cache/hyprland/hyprlandCrashReport756.txt`.
- **02:11:14–16** — uwsm restarts Hyprland; it comes back, then **falls back
  to safe mode** (stock layout, no bar/dock/cliphist).
- **02:13–02:16** — recovery (next section): claude live-loads our real
  `hyprland.lua`, restarts waybar, the dock, cliphist and
  scratchpad-keeper; `launch-desktop` reopens what launch.conf lists; Grok
  Bot and both chat screens come up; `comms-deck self-test` ends `all
  passed`.

## What survived and what died

**Survived untouched** (user services, not desktop clients):

- `comms-deck.service`, `push-courier.service`, `scratchpad-keeper.service`
  — all `active`, running since Wed 21:28. The OOM kills were confined to
  `app.slice`, so the deck never went down.
- `vibe-approver` (kept polling its dead kitty socket for a few minutes and
  recovered with the new one).

**Killed at 02:11**: every kitty window (three agent terminals had memory
peaks of 1.3 GiB, 1.1 GiB and 767 MiB over their 4h43m lives), the three
python3 hook processes, and grok-bot (496 MiB peak).

**Came back**: claude, grok, devin (terminal seats), alice (grok-bot), and
both chat screens. Back to a working deck by 02:16.

## Cause

Exhausted RAM with no swap, on a machine where the GPU shares system
memory. The terminal agents are multi-hundred-megabyte to gigabyte
processes on 7.5 GiB; an allocation spike at 02:11 tipped it over, and the
kernel's only lever was killing processes. It picked helpers first and then
grok-bot; once Hyprland's clients were gone, Hyprland died too. One line in
the kernel dump says the whole story:

    Free swap = 0kB, Total swap = 0kB

## Recommendations, in order of leverage

1. **Add swap** — even 4 GiB of zram would turn the next spike into a slow
   evening instead of a cascade of kills. The single cheapest fix for this
   exact failure mode.
2. **Watch the agents' memory** — an alert (or gentle restart) when a
   kitty passes ~1 GiB would keep the three-terminal baseline under half
   the RAM.
3. **Look at lumo** — 28 notes are waiting on a seat that isn't set up;
   either reconnect it (see `~/LUMO-RECONNECT.md`) or take it out of the
   roster so friends stop writing to it.
4. **Know that the load balancer was never installed**, so the deck's
   `tasks` tools error out — that predates this incident. Claude's v1
   implementation sits unmerged in `~/load-balancer-v1`; installing it
   (link + `systemctl --user restart comms-deck`) is one review away.
5. **Set the Discord webhook** if the mirror is wanted at all.

## What went right

`launch-desktop` and the deck did exactly what they promise. The deck
services shrugged off the OOM (they live outside `app.slice`), the queue
lost nothing, the launcher is idempotent — running it twice opens nothing
new — and the whole deck was back, in the right places, six minutes after
the crash. The outage cost only what the restart protocol (LAUNCH.md)
already pays for: the seats STOX commented out stayed down.
